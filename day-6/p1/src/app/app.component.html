Lesson 5 & 6

Angular Signals ⇄ RxJS Observables Interoperability

Angular 18 seamlessly bridges Signals and RxJS through @angular/core/rxjs-interop. Use Signals for synchronous state in templates, and Observables when you need complex async event pipelines.

toObservable(signal)

Converting Signals to Observables

Converts a reactive Signal into an Observable that emits every time the signal changes its value.

  • Enables RxJS operators (debounce, switchMap, distinctUntilChanged) on signal changes.
  • Runs inside Angular's effect phase.
  • Requires injection context or explicit Injector.
Signal: clickCount() = {{ clickCount() }} ➔ toObservable ➔ Mapped x2: {{ clickCountDoubled() }}
toSignal(observable$)

Converting Observables to Signals

Subscribes to an Observable and exposes the emitted values as a readable Signal in the template.

  • Automatically manages subscription and unsubscription on injection context destroy.
  • Can take an initialValue to avoid undefined.
  • Eliminates boilerplate | async pipes and manual onDestroy.
Observable: interval(1000) ➔ toSignal ➔ Current Tick: {{ intervalSignal() }}

Subject vs. BehaviorSubject (Multicasting)

Subjects act as both an Observable (you can subscribe to them) and an Observer (you can call .next() on them imperatively).

BehaviorSubject (Has Initial State)

State Holder: BehaviorSubject

Always stores the latest emitted value and immediately replays it to new subscribers.

Event Bus: Subject
